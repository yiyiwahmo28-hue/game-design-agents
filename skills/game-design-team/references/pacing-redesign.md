# Bring a choice earlier without hiding an economic change

Use this lens when a player must wait before making a consequential decision.
First establish whether waiting is the actual problem: it may serve learning,
anticipation, recovery or planning. Do not optimize every pause away.

## Freeze the comparison

Name the resource, starting stock, comparison horizon, arrival schedule and
all production channels. Distinguish gross production from spending and ending
balance. Where the request permits it, preserve the whole arrival schedule,
not only the final total. If a candidate front-loads income, call that a cash
flow change and include it in the explanation.

State what is allowed to change. Preserving production does not by itself
preserve prices, sinks, capacity, construction time or achievable progress.
Choose and document the additional constraints relevant to this task.

## Diagnose the gate

Locate the first usable action and the first choice that changes a relevant
outcome. They need not be the same moment. Find what prevents either one:
affordability, bundled purchases, a prerequisite, missing information, an
unavailable destination, or lack of a useful alternative.

Compare a few mechanisms rather than changing every parameter at once. Options
can include a functional partial purchase, parallel access, a different unlock
order or clearer information. A cheaper full purchase changes the sink. A free
reward changes production. A down payment with no usable effect may add clicks
without adding a decision. Explain why the selected mechanism addresses the
identified gate.

## Establish a consequential choice

Compare actions from the same known state. Show what each option enables and
what the player forgoes. Seek a relevant change in state or objective that
reverses the preferred action. If one action dominates every alternative under
the stated objective, do not call every available button a meaningful choice.

For a small deterministic model, enumerate outcomes when practical and retain
witness traces. For a larger or stochastic model, use justified scenarios and
state sampling limits. Never imply that selected favorable traces are an
exhaustive result. Include delayed or incorrect allocations, not only ideal
play. Keep game progression outcomes distinct from spendable currency.

## Show the cost of the redesign

An earlier branch can require more explanations, construction actions or
planning, and make a mistake costly sooner. Waiting may move to the middle of
the loop. A retained simple route may be economically dominated. Describe these
costs along with the benefit; decide whether they fit the audience and scope.

## Verify at the appropriate level

Run the actual before/after rules with the same horizon and starting inputs.
Check income at each relevant point, conservation, mission/upgrade effects,
invalid actions and end conditions. Ensure the interface reads real costs and
consequences from the execution path. Preserve the old version and document
changes in behavior, not merely updated wording.

These checks support statements about opportunity, timing and consequences.
Observe people using both versions to assess whether the earlier choice is
understandable and preferable. Do not convert model turns into human waiting
time or infer retention from a state trace. This lens defines no universal
ideal first-choice time, currency amount or reward curve.
