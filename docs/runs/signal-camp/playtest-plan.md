# Human playtest plan — not yet executed

This is a proposed follow-up, not a report of participants or results. The code
checks and assistant browser observations do not replace these observations.

## Questions

1. Before acting, can a new player explain why choosing the beacon or mapping
   tools first changes the outcome under the selected mission?
2. Does receiving a consequence on the second turn make the initial sequence
   feel more purposeful, despite waiting still occurring later?
3. Is the first decision legible enough, or does the earlier deadline introduce
   more pressure and mistakes than the original teaching sequence?

## Small formative session

Invite a small number of people from the intended audience (game designers or
independent developers). Record relevant experience rather than inventing a
representative player profile. Obtain permission before retaining quotations
or identifiable recordings.

Give each participant both versions; alternate presentation order to reduce
learning and novelty effects. Use the same disclosed objective, initial stock
and six-turn horizon for the pair. Then change the objective and observe
whether the participant changes their first allocation without coaching.

Ask the participant to state their plan before the first action and explain
the foregone result after the run. Record attempted actions, requests for help,
unexpected outcomes and the reason for any preference. Keep actual elapsed
time separate from model turns. Do not describe an optimal model policy as a
participant's expected behavior.

## Decision rules

- If people cannot predict the tradeoff, revise feedback or the decision's
  timing before adding more branches.
- If one tool appears universally best to participants, distinguish a display
  failure from a preferred objective or a model mistake.
- If both versions feel equally like waiting, inspect what is learned or
  changed by the early mission, rather than merely increasing click count.
- Keep negative observations and contradictory preferences in the case.

No retention lift, statistical preference or population-wide success rate can
be inferred from a small formative session.
